# Competitor information-architecture evidence v1

Status: RESEARCH EVIDENCE ONLY
Governing SSOT: `docs/ssot/M55_METHOD_AND_AUTHORITY_SSOT_v1.md`

## Scope and limits

This document records **structural observations** about how self-understanding
products in adjacent categories organise a method explanation. It exists to
answer one question: *which information does a reader look for before trusting a
method, and in what order?*

Hard limits, non-negotiable:

- No competitor wording is quoted or paraphrased into product copy.
- No competitor visual assets, layouts, colour systems or typography are copied.
- No competitor theory name, framework name or trademark is used in M55 copy.
- No competitor claim is adopted. A competitor claiming validation does not make
  the claim available to M55.
- Nothing in this document raises M55's authority level. Level changes require
  M55's own evidence, per the method SSOT.

Categories observed at a structural level only: personality-type questionnaires,
strengths-assessment products, birth-date-based reading services, and coaching
report products.

## Structural findings

### F1 — Method position

Products that read as trustworthy place the method explanation **before** the
price, not after. When the method sits below the purchase block, the reader
reaches the price without a frame for what they are buying.

M55 response: the four-step model on HOME sits between the general value
explanation and the Premium value comparison; `/dtr/lp` shows the free/Premium
difference before plan selection.

### F2 — Input transparency

The strongest structural pattern is an explicit list of *what is used as input*.
Readers treat an unlisted input as a hidden input.

M55 response: `/how-m55-works` section 2 lists all nine composition inputs in
daily Japanese, and states that no other-user comparison or externally acquired
data is used.

### F3 — Separation of stable and current

Products that mix a fixed trait model with a current-state questionnaire tend to
blur the two, which invites the reader to dismiss the whole as horoscope. Products
that keep them visibly separate read as more careful.

M55 response: the separation is the first step of the model and the first section
of the method page, and is the subject of the canonical explanation sentence.

### F4 — Reproducibility framed as consistency, not accuracy

Where a product explains that the same input yields the same output, readers treat
it as a fairness/consistency signal. Where the same fact is dressed as accuracy,
it invites scepticism the product cannot answer.

M55 response: the canonical reproducibility sentence describes processing
consistency, and the method page states explicitly that it is not an accuracy
claim.

### F5 — Explicit non-goals increase trust

A short "what this does not do" section is common in the more credible products
and consistently appears near the end rather than buried in legal text.

M55 response: section 10 of `/how-m55-works`, plus the canonical boundary
sentence repeated in compact placements.

### F6 — Depth difference expressed as scope, not quality

Tiered products that describe the paid tier as *more accurate* read as upselling.
Tiered products that describe it as *covering more ground* read as honest.

M55 response: the free/Premium difference copy states that the readable range
increases and explicitly denies that hit rate improves.

### F7 — One canonical method page

Products with several overlapping "how it works" pages produce contradictory
statements over time.

M55 response: `/how-m55-works` is the single canonical method route; the verifier
fails if a second method route appears.

### F8 — Privacy adjacency

Readers who accept a birth-date input look for the storage and sharing statement
in the same place as the method, not only in the privacy policy.

M55 response: section 9 of `/how-m55-works` states the login-free free reading,
the save behaviour, and that share cards carry neither the date of birth nor the
raw answers.

## Non-adoptions

Observed and deliberately **not** adopted:

- validation/accuracy badges and percentage figures
- named psychological frameworks as authority
- user-count or participant-count social proof
- expert-supervision claims without documented supervision
- future-prediction or other-person-feeling framing

Each of these is a LEVEL 2 or LEVEL 3 item under the method SSOT and remains
unavailable to M55 until M55's own evidence exists.
